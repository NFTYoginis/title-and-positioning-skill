# First-time development

For a manuscript with no published title yet — complete or near-complete, with a real thesis on the page, not a pitch. Read directly from `GabeYoga-HQ/Detox Book/TITLE-CANDIDATES.md` (marketing-claude, 2026-06-08/09).

## The shape of a round

Every round is **5 candidates**, each with:

1. **A main title + subtitle.**
2. **"Why it works"** — the specific case for this candidate: what thesis line it carries, what tone it signals, what curiosity-gap or credibility beat it creates. Not generic praise — tied to the book's actual content.
3. **"Risk"** — the honest downside, stated as plainly as the case for it. Miscategorization risk, tonal mismatch, over-abstraction, over-claiming — whatever the real exposure is. A candidate with no stated risk hasn't been evaluated, only generated.

## A stated north star to beat

Before generating, name the standard the round has to clear. The real trail's own line: *"keep that soul... then beat it."* The north star is a previously-liked option (often from an earlier, informal pass) — the job of a new round is not to replace it with something unrelated, it's to either refine it or genuinely surpass it. Carry the north star forward into the recommendation section explicitly — don't let it quietly disappear once new candidates exist.

## Constraints honored

State the book's real constraints before generating, and hold every candidate to them. From the real trail: voice (a specific author's established register), no-hype language rules (no "cleanse," no "miracle," no "transform in X days" — whatever the book's own honesty argument requires), and self-containment (no cross-reference to other books in a series unless the brief says otherwise). A candidate that violates a stated constraint doesn't make the round, even if it's a strong title on its own terms.

## The recommendation

Never a single winner. Name three, explicitly:

- **The safe pick** — closest to the north star, lowest risk, most likely to work as pitched.
- **The boldest commercial swing** — highest curiosity-gap, highest risk, the option that sells hardest to a cold browser if the rest of the package (cover, positioning) supports it.
- **The dark horse** — the most distinctive option, not necessarily the safest or the boldest, named because it's the most genuinely unlike anything else in the round.

State the trade-off for each in one line, not a re-explanation of its why-works/risk pair.

## Round 2 — the re-entry trigger

A second round is not "more options because the operator wants choices." In the real trail, Round 2 happened because **the manuscript itself evolved** — its own line: *"the manuscript evolved into its true subject... these five lean into the evolved book."* The book's actual thesis changed between rounds (from a narrower framing to a broader one — "a book about hearing yourself, disguised as a detox book").

Before running a new round, confirm what specifically changed in the manuscript's thesis. If nothing changed and the ask is just "give me different options," that's still a legitimate ask — but say so explicitly rather than implying the book evolved when it didn't. The re-entry condition matters because it tells you whether the new round should build on the same constraints and north star (nothing changed) or restate them against the new thesis (something did).

## Worked reference — the real Round 1 → Round 2 shape (*Are You Actually Hungry?*)

Round 1 produced 5 candidates spanning a range: sharp/curiosity-gap, plainspoken/teacher-direct, poetic/Sufi register, closest-evolution-of-the-liked-line, and the boldest reframe-as-title swing. The recommendation named the closest-evolution candidate as the safe pick, the reframe-as-title as the boldest commercial swing, and the plainspoken option as the dark horse.

Round 2's five leaned into the evolved thesis (listening, common sense, curiosity — with the original subject now understood as the vehicle, not the point) and drew from the manuscript's own protected lines rather than inventing new imagery. None repeated Round 1. The top pick raised the single most-quoted line in the manuscript to the title position — distinctive, already visually native to work already built on that sentence, and calm on a spine. The bolder alternative was a true pattern-interrupt (a book in the category disowning the category's usual assumption from the cover).

The eventual locked title — *Are You Actually Hungry? The Missing Conversation About Detox, Wellness, and Common Sense* — took Round 1's plainspoken/teacher-direct candidate as its main title, paired with a final subtitle written specifically to frame the whole evolved book (listening, common sense) rather than reused verbatim from either round's own ten candidates. A reminder that the "winner" doesn't have to be one candidate whole-cloth, and that a locked title can still need one more real pass after the round that produced its main title — the safe/bold/dark-horse framing surfaces real trade-offs, it doesn't guarantee the final answer is any single listed candidate.
